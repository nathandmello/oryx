# Work with stakeholder profiles

Use the [profile guide](../../../playbook/stakeholder-profiles.md) and [conversation guidance](../../../playbook/conversation.md). The PM owns every saved sentence and decision.

## Get to know a person

Offer this walkthrough after the parent product and any chosen area walkthrough. A PM can also ask for it during ordinary work.
Let the PM skip, pause, or return. Follow their partial answers. For an explicit request, honour the requested depth.

Start with one person. If the PM named their support lead, use that detail.
Ask what the person needs from the next interaction. Confirm which sources the PM allows and where a reviewed point would go.
Match the person's name, aliases and current role in the private identity directory before opening their profile.
Consult the relevant [stakeholder map](../../../playbook/stakeholders.md) entry when the decision role matters.

> “What did Jo ask you to bring to the rollout review?”

Follow the answer with one question. Ask for an example when a phrase like “cares about quality” leaves the work unclear.
Preserve the source, scope, exceptions and the PM's uncertainty. Leave unanswered topics open.

When the PM asks for a preview, show one exact sentence, its source and destination. Wait for their approval.
Preview their corrections again. One approved observation can complete the interview.
A one-person request can leave a skipped walkthrough skipped.

If the PM wants to resume later, preview a private handoff with the next question and expected output.
Save it with their approval. At return, resolve the intended person and check the current record.
Keep unapproved claims and source excerpts out of the handoff.

## Notice evidence during other work

Use the [ingestion workflow](../../../playbook/ingestion.md) for passages authorised for stakeholder review.
Limit each read to the permitted source, purpose and coverage. Respect off-record, chat-only and source-specific restrictions before assessment.

The subscriber uses the original speaker, exact passage and source locator. Keep its evidence reference through preview and acceptance.
An attendee list, forwarded copy or job title supplies no working preference.
Ask when two people fit a name or two observations conflict. Keep a temporary position with its initiative.

Keep the current task moving. At a natural pause, including between steps, present one selected offer with its source and concrete benefit.
Record presentation with `pm.py offer presented TOKEN`. Use `pm.py offer decline TOKEN` when the PM declines.

Respect quiet mode and the shared offer limit. Defer competing opportunities through the shared selector.
When asked to silence suggestions, save the [scoped preference](../../../playbook/overrides.md) immediately.
Respect the PM's privacy if they decline to explain. Public feedback needs separate approval of its exact text.

## Use a profile in the work

For a named reader, establish the purpose, subject and decision sought. Retrieve current accepted entries for the intended audience.
For an ambiguous panel, clarify the unresolved person before using anyone's profile.
Check decision rights in the scoped map. Check a current position in the relevant initiative and its cited evidence.
Treat a preference from a former role, a superseded claim and an expired review as historical.

A PM's current correction can guide this conversation immediately. Ask separately before updating the saved record.
If profiles are unavailable, work from the permitted details. The PM can use the hub without building a profile.

For an update, agenda or decision request, tailor the order, depth and evidence.
Cite consequential inferences in chat. Keep personal notes, guesses and coaching citations out of the stakeholder-facing draft.
The PM decides what to share. On follow-up edits, recheck the current audience and scope.
Ask when a pronoun could refer to more than one person.

## Rehearse a conversation

Start with the proposal, the audience, the decision sought, and the PM's concern. Use relevant profiles and current project evidence.
If context is thin, name the gap and offer a scenario using only the supplied details.
For each person, ground a possible spoken response or objection in a specific observation. Mark imagined dialogue as hypothetical.
If asked what someone might privately think, offer a plausible concern as a question the PM could test.

Offer the evidence or clarification that could address the strongest objection. For a panel, identify shared concerns and real differences.
Suggest a conversation order when a dependency or decision role supports it. Leave the call with the PM.
If every reaction sounds alike, check whether one account shaped the whole rehearsal.

Close with one question for the PM or the stakeholder that would reduce the uncertainty. Keep rehearsal output in chat.
Only an actual interaction can supply new evidence about a person's response or position.
