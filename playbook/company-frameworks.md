# Evidence and frameworks for company context

Visit each of the five topics during the company walkthrough. The examples suggest where to look after the PM describes their sources.

| Question | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Who are the customers, and what value does each receive? | Research or support evidence for experienced value. A company deck can establish the intended segment and promise. Distinguish the buyer, user and beneficiary when they differ. |
| How does the company make money, and what drives its economics? | Pricing establishes the offer. Finance or billing records can establish realised revenue, concentration and costs within a stated period. |
| How do products work together? | A portfolio map or observed customer journey can show how two offerings depend on each other. Verify whether customers actually use them together. |
| What drives growth and competitive advantage? | Cohorts, channel records and win/loss evidence can explain repeat acquisition, retention or purchasing choices. A growth rate alone leaves the mechanism open. |
| Where are the opportunities, risks and strategic trade-offs? | Approved plans, operating reviews, commitments or constraints can identify competing choices. Attribute any ranking and the reason to its owner. |

## Coach from the evidence

Use a framework only when it helps explain the business or surface one material uncertainty. Tailor follow-ups to the PM's words and Studio preferences.

| Framework | When to use it | Follow-up or common misuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business Model Canvas | When segment, offer and economics are unclear, trace who benefits, who pays and what delivery costs. Scope each materially different business model. | “Who pays for that service?” A published price alone cannot establish margin. [Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas) |
| Five Forces | Define the industry and the pressure on how value is divided. Look for buyer or supplier power, substitutes, entrants or rivalry. | “What do customers use if they switch away?” Claims of durable advantage need comparative evidence. [Harvard Business School](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx) |
| Growth Loops | For a proposed loop, trace one cycle and the output reinvested in the next. | “What happens after that referral?” Keep a documented acquisition funnel as such until evidence shows how its output returns. [Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops) |
| Strategy Choice Cascade | Look for an aspiration, choices about where to play and how to win, and the capabilities and systems they require. | “Has leadership chosen that segment?” A market opportunity can remain an option. [Roger Martin](https://rogerlmartin.com/thought-pillars/strategy) |
| Product Strategy Stack | Trace a product's intended contribution to company strategy and mission. Later, link roadmap choices and product goals. | “Which company priority does that support?” A roadmap item alone cannot establish strategy. [Ghadiyali and Mehta](https://www.ravi-mehta.com/product-strategy-stack/) |
