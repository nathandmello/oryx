# Evidence and frameworks for company context

Visit each topic once. Pick the follow-up from what the person can speak to for that part of the business.

| Company topic | What a founder starting out might contribute | What a PM in an established business might contribute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customers and value | Who they hope to serve first, why that market matters to them, and the value they intend to offer. | Segments served, buyers and beneficiaries, and the value the business delivers to each. |
| Business model and economics | Who might pay, a price under consideration, an actual pilot payment, or a cost they already incur. | The pricing model, which products earn revenue, and the costs they know. Billing or Finance can establish figures within a stated period. |
| Products and their relationships | The role of the first offering in the business, including how a manual service supports an early product. | How the portfolio delivers value and where customers use offerings together. Link individual product briefs. |
| Growth and competitive advantage | How they met an early prospect, what that person uses today, and why they think someone might switch. | Which channels bring in customers, what keeps them, and why deals are won or lost. Scope claims to the available evidence. |
| Opportunities, risks and trade-offs | The choice the founder faces now, what constrains it, and which assumption could change their mind. | Current company priorities, investments or constraints, who chose them, and what remains open beyond the PM’s remit. |

## Interpret the answer in context

Use the examples to listen and select one follow-up. A startup can have paying customers. An established company can still be exploring a new market.

If someone says “I don’t know,” ask “Has that been decided yet?” when the distinction matters.
An unmade pricing decision belongs in the brief as an open choice if the founder wants to record it.
If pricing exists and the PM lacks visibility, capture the limit of their knowledge. Offer to check a source they identify.

“We haven't charged anyone yet” establishes the founder's account of revenue to date.
“I think clinics would pay” is a hypothesis. Preserve the quote and name the founder's hypothesis in `interpretation`.
If the founder agrees, use `uncertainty` to name the question that remains.
A later remark from one clinic belongs to that conversation. Ask what it changes before revising the brief.

A founder’s experience, access to prospective buyers, or a partner’s commitment can explain why a market is within reach. [UVA Darden](https://www.darden.virginia.edu/effectuation)
When a company has one product, apply the same scope test. A paid pilot can inform a commercial hypothesis.
Usage or repeat purchases can inform a product-market fit assessment for that offering and segment. Preserve the period and the limits of the evidence.

## Coach from the evidence

Use a framework only when it helps explain the business or surface one material uncertainty. Tailor follow-ups to the PM's words and Studio preferences.

For founder intake, borrow two habits from gstack. Use one follow-up only when it clarifies what to record.

- **Office Hours.** Pin down the buyer, their current alternative, or a commitment the founder mentions. “Three clinics are interested” might prompt “What have those clinics agreed to so far?” Preserve introductions, unpaid trials and purchases on their own terms. [Startup diagnostic](https://github.com/nathandmello/gstack/blob/702a1a9b698080aca72503f5feed9ed8cd552348/office-hours/sections/phase-2a-startup-diagnostic.md#L81-L110)
- **CEO Review.** Clarify what a number counts or what an existing trade-off means. If enterprise sales are on hold, ask “What led to that decision?” Preserve the founder’s reason. [CEO review principles](https://github.com/nathandmello/gstack/blob/702a1a9b698080aca72503f5feed9ed8cd552348/plan-ceo-review/SKILL.md#L536-L543)

Use answers already given. An unknown can stand after the founder reviews the topic.

**For later workflows.** Observed workarounds, prototype use and surprising behavior can inform product context.
Choosing a first paid offering, comparing target markets or testing a future market thesis belongs in strategy.
When the founder has already made such a choice, company intake can document its current business scope and their stated reason.

| Framework | When to use it | Follow-up or common misuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business Model Canvas | When segment, offer and economics are unclear, trace who benefits, who pays and what delivery costs. Scope each materially different business model. | “Who pays for that service?” A published price alone cannot establish margin. [Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas) |
| Five Forces | Define the industry and the pressure on how value is divided. Look for buyer or supplier power, substitutes, entrants or rivalry. | “What do customers use if they switch away?” Claims of durable advantage need comparative evidence. [Harvard Business School](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx) |
| Growth Loops | For a proposed loop, trace one cycle and the output reinvested in the next. | “What happens after that referral?” Keep a documented acquisition funnel as such until evidence shows how its output returns. [Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops) |
| Strategy Choice Cascade | Look for an aspiration, choices about where to play and how to win, and the capabilities and systems they require. | “Has leadership chosen that segment?” A market opportunity can remain an option. [Roger Martin](https://rogerlmartin.com/thought-pillars/strategy) |
| Product Strategy Stack | Trace a product's intended contribution to company strategy and mission. Later, link roadmap choices and product goals. | “Which company priority does that support?” A roadmap item alone cannot establish strategy. [Ghadiyali and Mehta](https://www.ravi-mehta.com/product-strategy-stack/) |
