# Writing technical instructions and PM prose

## Technical instructions

Use ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE), Issue 9, dated 15 January 2025. Consult Part 1 for the writing rules and Part 2 for the dictionary. [Publisher's full standard](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)

1. Identify procedures and descriptions. Review the applicable rules for each passage.
2. Check technical words against the dictionary for approved spelling, part of speech, meaning, and form.
3. Apply the technical noun and technical verb rules to necessary domain terms. Use each term consistently.
4. Give an instruction in the imperative. Put necessary conditions before the action.
5. Give one instruction per sentence, except when the actions must occur together.
6. Check procedural sentences against the 20-word limit and descriptive sentences against the 25-word limit.
7. Use the standard's Section 8 counting rules. Review paragraph structure, warnings, and notes under their applicable sections.

For dictionary use, see rules 1.1–1.4. For procedures and descriptions, see Sections 5 and 6. The publisher controls the full text and its reproduction. [Official distribution](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html)

oryx checks approximate word counts, paragraph length, and a few house terms. A reviewer must check approved meanings, syntax, technical terminology, and exceptions against the standard.

Record the passages and rules actually checked before reporting a standards review. A passing oryx check alone supports no claim of full STE compliance.

## Hemingway and meaning

Use concrete nouns, active verbs, short sentences, and necessary detail. Hemingway described omission as a narrative technique that depends on the writer knowing the omitted material. [Hemingway's Paris Review interview](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4825/the-art-of-fiction-no-21-ernest-hemingway)

Hemingway left no formal technical writing specification. Hemingway Editor flags hard sentences, passive constructions, and other possible edits. Review each change for meaning. [Editor's published guidance](https://hemingwayapp.com/help/docs/highlighted-issues)

Keep negation, obligation, conditions, uncertainty, and ownership explicit in PM records. Preserve quotations verbatim. Keep the actor and each distinct commitment in summaries.

> We will not enter the data centre business or create or own LLM models. We will rent capacity as required and use existing models.

Both sentences belong in the company constraints. Removing the first loses a strategic exclusion. Changing “may” to “will” can turn an option into a commitment.

The 800-word limit in oryx applies to authored prose. Edit repetition first. Apply [the filing test](filing.md) before creating another record.
