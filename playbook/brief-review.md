# Review briefs and hypotheses

## Establish the decision

State the choice, decision owner, and commitment requested. If the brief leaves a material part implicit, report that gap.
Match evidence to the stage. A discovery brief can justify a bounded test while the case for delivery remains open.

Apply [the numerical evidence procedure](numbers.md) to material baselines, forecasts, rates, and results. Record the comparison basis and unresolved gaps.

Describe the observed problem before naming the proposed solution. State the strongest competing explanation. Compare credible options against the same criteria, including the current approach when viable. Name the assumption that would change the decision if it failed. [Strategic choice and tests](https://hbr.org/2012/09/bringing-science-to-the-art-of-strategy)

## Check what each sentence commits to

| Lens | Strategy brief | Initiative brief |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | Name the priority customers and relevant workflow. | Name the affected users. Identify the buyer or beneficiary if different. |
| What | Name the priority problem and the trade-off in focus or resources. | State the proposed change, first increment, and exclusions. |
| Why | Diagnose the challenge and explain why this choice serves the business goal. | Trace the observed problem to the customer outcome and its business value. |
| When | State the horizon, consequence of waiting, and reason to revisit. | Explain the order, dependencies, and when enough evidence will be available for the next decision. |
| How | Explain the mechanism and capabilities the strategy depends on. | Explain how the intervention changes the workflow. Name the owner, rollout, and outcome measure. |

Check that the problem names the actor, task, situation, and observable signal. Keep the suspected cause explicit as an inference.
Replace vague verbs when readers could infer different actions or outcomes. Preserve actors, scope, conditions, uncertainty, and accepted decisions in edits.

Link the customer outcome to the business goal. For a measurable commitment, check the metric, baseline, target, horizon, and guardrails.
If the baseline is missing, name its source or how to establish it. State which decision can proceed.

Assess value, usability, feasibility, and viability separately. Ask the relevant owners about material risks. Treat a failed mandatory constraint as a blocker for commitment. [Four product risks](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/)

## Match the test to the hypothesis

| Claim | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- |
| The problem occurs and matters | Recent examples, workflow records, frequency, and consequences for the target segment. |
| People will choose or buy the offer | Observed choice or a real commitment under realistic terms. |
| People can use it | Representative users attempting the task. Observe completion, errors, and workarounds. |
| The change improves the outcome | A credible comparison against what would otherwise happen. Use random assignment when appropriate. |
| The business can deliver and sustain it | Representative delivery evidence, resource use, failure rates, and costs. |

Check the population, expected result, method, primary measure, window, and any comparison the claim needs.
Specify the smallest result worth acting on. Record the response to supportive, adverse, and inconclusive results before testing.
Define side-effect limits and confirm that the test can detect a meaningful change when estimating impact. [Experiment design](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-pre-experiment-stage/)

Check that the claim, measure, and decision criterion refer to the same outcome. Assess sources for directness, relevance, and limits.
Ask which competing explanation would also fit the result. Limit the conclusion to what the evidence can distinguish. [Test and Learn](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book/test-and-learn-html)

### Worked example

“Improve renewal engagement with an AI reminder” assumes a solution and leaves the outcome open. Email opens show interaction. The team needs renewal evidence to decide whether reminders help customers renew on time.

“Among eligible multi-seat accounts, earlier reminders will increase on-time renewals by eight percentage points versus the current schedule.” The threshold is illustrative. Define eligibility, the renewal window, and acceptable unsubscribe rates before testing. Check that the available cohort can support the decision.

## Report the review

Give a verdict for the stated decision. Use **Ready**, **Revise**, or **Reframe**. Use Reframe when the draft asks the wrong question. Use Revise when a resolvable gap blocks the current decision.

Cite the passage or missing claim. Explain what decision it puts at risk. Give the smallest useful correction or evidence request and its owner.
An unresolved decision, unsupported diagnosis presented as fact, or test of a different claim can block approval regardless of prose quality.
