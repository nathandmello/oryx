# Product strategy framework

Source: Nathan's framework, supplied in this conversation on 2026-11-18. This file preserves its method for future work.

## Product context and remit

For product work, use the PM’s [confirmed remit](context.md#pm-remit) and their reviewed company and product context.
Trace a customer outcome to the product's contribution. Clarify decision rights when they matter.

## Strategy

Strategy connects business needs to chosen problems and a high-level solution hypothesis. Explain why the choice matters and how it can help.

Goals, metrics, features, roadmaps, budgets, and mission statements contribute to strategy. The strategy explains the choices between them.

Review four risks separately:

| Risk | Lead | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value | Product | Will customers buy or use it? |
| Usability | Design | Can customers use it successfully? |
| Feasibility | Engineering | Can the team build it with its time, skills, and technology? |
| Viability | Product and business owners | Does it fit business, operational, financial, and compliance constraints? |

Record blockers separately. An agent prepares questions and evidence; the responsible people make their judgments.

Value validation connects the customer and problem, the solution's effect, and a test of the underlying hypotheses.

## Strategy brief

- **Why** gives the context, business need, and agreed goals or gaps.
- **Who** identifies the priority customers and their problems.
- **What** names the problem to pursue first, supporting evidence, and priority rationale.
- **When** gives the decision horizon, reason to act, and review trigger.
- **How** explains the high-level approach, expected effect, and measures it can influence.

Use [the brief-review checks](brief-review.md) to assess the frame, wording, and evidence.

Keep user stories, detailed flows, and engineering specifications in the configured delivery system.

## Goal alignment

Before starting product strategy, check which company objective the product should support and what establishes its current priority.
If that objective is unclear, explain the gap and offer to clarify it with the PM first.
Unrelated gaps can wait. A completed onboarding pass preserves the record of what the PM already reviewed.

An outcome has five parts: metric, baseline, target, time horizon, and guardrails.

Use [the metric registry](metrics.md) for the effective calculation and population.

Connect organisation goals, product goals, customer outcomes, initiative goals, and feature goals where those levels apply. Metrics measure these outcomes.

In an alignment session, separate proposed solutions, customer outcomes, and organisational objectives. Trace proposed solutions back to their problem and intended outcome.

If a company deck describes a market as an opportunity, ask whether leadership has chosen to pursue it.
Carry the original wording and open question into strategy and planning until the PM can establish the choice.
Ask the PM which outcome the product can influence. Preserve the source of official targets and the PM's rationale for proposed ones.

## Problems and evidence

Use this problem statement:

> [Who] struggles to [do what] when [situation], and we see it in [observable signal].

Quantitative evidence identifies patterns, scale, and metric changes. Qualitative evidence adds behaviour, context, and possible explanations.

Choose a research method for the question. Generative research explores the problem; evaluative research assesses an approach; experiments test a hypothesis.

Maintain three categories: Know, Think, and Curious. Know needs a source. Think records assumptions. Curious records unknowns.

Identify the assumption whose failure most threatens the decision. Record contradictory evidence and sampling limits.

## Hypotheses and tests

Use four statements:

- **We believe** states one claim about a specific population or condition.
- **We will test it by** states the method and any comparison.
- **We will measure** defines the primary measure, window, and relevant guardrails.
- **We proceed if** links the evidence threshold to a decision.

Assess criticality, test cost, evidence quality, and time to a useful result. Prefer a small test that fits the question.

Record the criterion before the test. Preserve amendments and separate supported, contradicted, and inconclusive results.

Use Nathan's evidence scale as a reference:

| Score | Evidence category |
| --- | --- |
| 0–1 | Self-conviction |
| 2 | Estimates and plans |
| 3 | Anecdotal evidence |
| 4–5 | Market data |
| 6–7 | Customer and user evidence |
| 8–9 | Test results |
| 10 | Launch and usage data |

A category records evidence origin. Judge confidence against the specific claim and decision. Check directness, quality, age, and limits. Model agreement adds no customer evidence.

## Iterations

Describe roadmap items through intended outcomes. Explain their order, expected value, confidence, dependencies, and cost.

Choose the structure for the audience: `Now / Next / Later`, `Must / Should / Could`, `Crawl / Walk / Run`, or `Bones / Muscle / Brain`.

Use [the opportunity workflow](opportunities.md) to move from an evaluated problem to an investment decision.

Use results to revisit earlier choices. Preserve rejected alternatives and the reason for their rejection when that context remains useful.
